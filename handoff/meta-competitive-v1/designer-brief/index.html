Context

Who the ads are for

Credo Legal is a consumer debt-defense law firm. Its attorneys make debt collectors prove a debt is valid before anyone pays: a written validation request under the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA §1692g). Clients get a free case review and pay a flat monthly fee. Credo is not a debt-settlement company, which shapes what the ads can promise.

The audience is people being chased by collectors or debt buyers over credit-card or medical debt. They are stressed, short on time and wary of anything that feels like a pitch.

The campaign runs on Facebook and Instagram with a lead objective, under Meta's Credit special ad category. Every ad needs the responsible attorney's approval before it runs.

What we've learned

Signals from the first rounds

Reactions to what we've made so far, mostly from Vernon. Treat them as input, not rules.

Light read better than dark.The black-background versions were hard to read. The light versions of S1 came across as "significantly better".

Dark isn't off the table, but it has to earn its legibility.

FDR's ads feel more graphic than ours.Ours read as "just text, or text and images"; FDR's use "tables, diagrams, graphics, colors".

The biggest gap to close, and where there's the most room to invent.

Lots of red got heavy.Red bars, red tags and red buttons all at once made the dark set feel dense.

Red is Credo's colour. How much to use is your call.

Photos felt too polished.Earlier imagery looked "too elegant and high end" for this audience.

Everyday and relatable has landed better.

With or without a photo both worked.The two light S1 tests, one with a photo and one without, were both well received.

Photos are a choice per concept, not a requirement.

1:11080 × 1080Facebook and Instagram feedsThe most common feed placement.

9:161080 × 1920Stories and ReelsMeta's app interface covers the hatched areas: top 14% (≈270px), bottom 35% (≈670px) and 6% each side (≈65px). Anything important placed there may be hidden. Check in the Ads Manager preview.
16:91920 × 1080Landscape placementsMeta's usual landscape image is 1.91:1; 16:9 is supported but may crop in some placements.

A note on legibility. Feed images show about 400px wide on a phone, so text that looks fine on a 1080px canvas can end up tiny. In our drafts the disclaimer rendered around 5px on screen. Worth checking every size at phone scale. Export PNG (or JPG at quality 90+), sRGB, under 30 MB per file.

Draft copy

The words we have so far

Each concept has draft copy that has been through a first legal read. Use it as raw material: cut it, re-order it, split it across a layout, or suggest better lines. Any change to the wording goes back through legal review before it ships, so flag what you changed. Credo's voice is calm and specific, without emojis or exclamation marks. Guardrails

The few fixed constraints

These come from attorney-advertising rules and Meta's policies, so they aren't creative choices.

Required on every image, every size

Attorney Advertising. Prior outcomes don't guarantee similar results. Credo Legal Services, P.C. Responsible attorney Jack [CONFIRM], NY & NJ. Not legal advice.

Style and placement are up to you, as long as it's readable at phone size and never cropped. The wording may still change, so leave a little room.

Always

  • The disclaimer on every image, readable and uncropped
  • The real logo, from the supplied files
  • Rating figures kept as editable text, so the placeholders are quick to update
  • Illustrated bills, letters and documents kept generic: no real names, account numbers or amounts
  • Wording changes flagged for legal review

Never

  • Testimonials: review quotes, client stories, or people presented as real clients
  • Savings, payment or outcome figures and promises ("$438/mo", "debt-free", "settle for less")
  • Anything that could be mistaken for a Freedom Debt Relief ad
